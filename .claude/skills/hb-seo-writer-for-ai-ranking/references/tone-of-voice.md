# **Tone of Voice Guide**

## **Overall Brand Tone Analysis**

### **Core Brand Voice**

The brand voice is **informative, practical, authoritative, and visitor-focused**. It serves as a trusted local guide whose primary objective is helping visitors plan successfully, avoid problems, and maximize their experience.

The tone prioritizes **clarity over creativity**, delivering essential information in a straightforward manner while maintaining an approachable and helpful personality. The writing balances tourism promotion with operational guidance, ensuring readers are both inspired and well-prepared.

### **Key Characteristics**

#### **1\. Authoritative**

The voice communicates with certainty and confidence. Instructions, regulations, safety requirements, and logistical details are presented clearly and without ambiguity.

**Characteristics:**

* States facts directly  
* Avoids hedging language  
* Provides definitive guidance  
* References official rules and requirements confidently

#### **2\. Helpful and Service-Oriented**

The writing consistently anticipates visitor questions and concerns before they arise.

**Characteristics:**

* Explains what visitors need to know  
* Provides practical recommendations  
* Addresses common challenges proactively  
* Helps readers make informed decisions

#### **3\. Informative and Detailed**

Information is comprehensive, organized, and actionable.

**Characteristics:**

* Includes specific operational details  
* Provides exact figures, distances, times, and costs  
* Eliminates uncertainty wherever possible  
* Prioritizes usefulness over brevity

#### **4\. Safety-Conscious**

Safety messaging is direct and prominent without becoming alarmist.

**Characteristics:**

* Clearly communicates risks  
* Explains consequences when necessary  
* Encourages preparedness  
* Maintains a responsible, educational tone

#### **5\. Encouraging and Promotional**

While primarily informative, the voice occasionally shifts into destination marketing mode.

**Characteristics:**

* Highlights nearby opportunities  
* Encourages exploration  
* Creates excitement around experiences  
* Uses aspirational travel language selectively

---

# **Dominant Emotional Tone**

### **Primary Emotion: Reassuring Confidence**

The brand creates confidence by giving visitors the information they need to navigate their experience successfully.

### **Secondary Emotional Traits**

#### **Practical Optimism**

The tone assumes visitors are excited about their trip and helps them make the most of it.

#### **Responsible Guidance**

The voice communicates expectations and restrictions in a firm but professional manner.

#### **Enthusiastic Discovery**

When discussing attractions and experiences, the tone becomes more aspirational and experience-focused.

# **Writing Principles**

## **1\. Lead With Practical Value**

Readers should quickly understand:

* What they need to know  
* What they need to do  
* What they should expect

Avoid lengthy introductions or unnecessary storytelling.

### **Preferred Approach**

"Advance reservations are strongly recommended."

Not:

"You may wish to consider making reservations in advance if possible."

---

## **2\. Be Specific**

The voice relies heavily on precise information.

### **Include:**

* Exact times  
* Distances  
* Fees  
* Capacities  
* Regulations  
* Requirements

### **Avoid:**

* Vague estimates  
* Ambiguous instructions  
* Generalized recommendations

---

## **3\. Anticipate Visitor Questions**

Content should proactively answer:

* How much?  
* When?  
* Where?  
* What happens if?  
* What should I bring?  
* What alternatives exist?

The voice functions as a knowledgeable local expert.

---

## **4\. Deliver Warnings Clearly**

Safety and operational restrictions are communicated directly.

### **Preferred Structure**

**Rule → Reason → Consequence**

Example framework:

"Parking is prohibited in this area. This roadway experiences heavy traffic. Violators may be fined or towed."

This approach feels informative rather than threatening.

---

## **5\. Promote Through Relevance**

Promotional language appears naturally within the visitor journey.

Rather than aggressive selling, recommendations are positioned as logical next steps or complementary experiences.

### **Preferred Framework**

"If you're visiting this attraction, consider exploring..."

Not:

"Don't miss this incredible opportunity\!"

---

# **Sentence Structure**

## **Preferred Sentence Style**

### **Direct and Efficient**

* Medium-length sentences  
* Clear subject-verb-object structure  
* Information-rich wording  
* Minimal fluff

### **Example Pattern**

"Visitors should arrive early to avoid crowds and warmer afternoon temperatures."

---

## **Use Lists Frequently**

The voice favors:

* Bullet points  
* Fee breakdowns  
* Requirements lists  
* Preparation checklists  
* Recommended actions

Lists improve readability and reinforce authority.

---

## **Use Transitional Guidance**

Frequently guide readers toward next actions.

Examples:

* "Visitors should be aware..."  
* "Please note..."  
* "Those planning a visit..."  
* "Advance reservations are recommended..."  
* "Should you prefer..."

---

# **Pacing**

## **Information Delivery**

The pace is steady and structured.

### **Typical Flow**

1. Important fact  
2. Supporting details  
3. Visitor implication  
4. Recommended action

This creates a logical progression that feels organized and trustworthy.

# **Words and Phrases to Avoid**

## **Overly Sales-Oriented Language**

Avoid:

* Amazing deal  
* Unbelievable  
* Once-in-a-lifetime  
* Best ever  
* Incredible savings  
* Must-buy

---

## **Excessively Casual Language**

Avoid:

* Super  
* Awesome  
* Crazy  
* Epic  
* Cool  
* No worries  
* You'll love it

---

## **Overly Corporate Language**

Avoid:

* Synergy  
* Stakeholders  
* Leveraging  
* Optimization strategy  
* Value proposition

---

## **Overly Emotional Language**

Avoid:

* Magical  
* Life-changing  
* Breathtaking beyond words  
* Mind-blowing  
* Extraordinary in every way

The voice prefers credibility over exaggeration.

# **Content Hierarchy**

When creating content in this voice, prioritize information in the following order:

### **1\. Essential Visitor Information**

Hours, access, costs, availability

### **2\. Rules and Requirements**

Policies, restrictions, regulations

### **3\. Preparation Guidance**

What to bring, weather, accessibility

### **4\. Visitor Recommendations**

Best times, planning suggestions

### **5\. Related Experiences**

Additional attractions, tours, activities

---

# **When Writing in This Tone of Voice, Consider the Following Details**

### **Always:**

✓ Provide actionable information

✓ Use exact figures whenever possible

✓ Anticipate common visitor concerns

✓ Present regulations clearly and confidently

✓ Prioritize visitor preparedness

✓ Organize information logically

✓ Balance authority with helpfulness

✓ Use promotional language sparingly and strategically

✓ Maintain factual accuracy

✓ Focus on practical outcomes

---

### **Never:**

✗ Overhype experiences

✗ Use excessive marketing language

✗ Be vague about requirements

✗ Minimize safety considerations

✗ Use slang or trendy expressions

✗ Assume prior knowledge

✗ Bury important operational information

✗ Create uncertainty around rules or policies

---

# **Copywriter Summary**

This voice should sound like a **trusted destination expert who knows exactly what visitors need to have a successful experience**. It is practical, authoritative, and visitor-focused. The goal is not to impress readers with clever writing but to equip them with useful information, build confidence, encourage preparedness, and guide them toward enjoyable experiences through clear, reliable communication.
